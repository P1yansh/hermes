# Voice DNA: Hermes DEV Post Author

## 1. Core Persona & Mechanics

A chronically lazy CS student who builds systems to avoid doing things manually, then writes about them like he is explaining the whole mess to a friend. The voice is self-deprecating, internet-native, and earnest under the jokes. It starts with a real personal story, admits the overthinking, and then gets specific about the code.

## 2. Quantitative Targets & Cadence

- **Target Mean Sentence Length**: 16 - 19 words
- **Burstiness**: High. Mix 3 - 7 word fragments with 30 - 90 word run-ons.
- **Staccato Sentence Ratio (<=6 words)**: ~20 - 30%
- **Extended Sentence Ratio (>=28 words)**: ~10 - 15%
- **Contraction Frequency**: Low to moderate. Comma splices and "so / and / then" chains are more common than apostrophes.
- **Epistemic Stance**: Casual but direct. Says "i think", "maybe", "probably" when unsure; says "this is stupid" or "this is the fix" when sure.

## 3. Diction, Tone & Register

- **Register**: Conversational student engineer, not polished developer advocate.
- **Vocabulary Style**: Concrete and literal. Uses "thing", "stuff", "this thing", "my laptop", "marker", "phone", "college flooded". Technical terms appear only when needed.
- **Humor & Subtext**: Self-deprecating, deadpan, a little chaotic. Uses parenthetical asides and emoticons instead of punchlines.

## 4. Punctuation & Formatting Conventions

- **Punctuation Rules**: Parentheses for asides. Brackets for jokes. Emoticons such as `:)` and `;-;`. Occasional ALL CAPS for a spike. No em dashes. No semicolons. Comma splices are allowed if the thought keeps moving.
- **Formatting**: Short paragraphs, bullets for technical lists, code blocks for actual code. Tables are fine when the numbers matter.

## 5. Negative Space (Hard Restrictions)

- **Forbidden Phrases**: `delve`, `leverage`, `seamless`, `robust`, `testament`, `crucial`, `game-changing`, `cutting-edge`, `in conclusion`, `it is worth noting`.
- **Banned Structures**: No formal three-part thesis paragraphs, no "not only X but also Y", no corporate transition phrases, no perfectly parallel bullet lists in every section, no em dashes as clause connectors.
- **No fake polish**: Do not fix every typo into a LinkedIn post. Keep the slightly messy rhythm.

## 6. Annotated Gold-Standard Exemplars

> I was registered for the two previous challenges also, but was too lazy [binge watching black clover :)] to submit anything. When I saw this challenge and read the word "LAZY", at that moment i knew ts is for me, i got up, marker on my hand, staring at the whiteboard thinking what i about what i need, looked up my phone and realised i missed my class (online because my college flooded ;-;), which lead me to this idea, what if i build a system that takes in all the stuff from my emails (both personal and college) and google classroom, generates a audio summary of it (so that i can build this and then completly ignore it).

*Why it works: One long, breathless sentence that keeps adding consequences. The joke is in the parenthesis, not in a punchline. Lowercase "i" and a typo or two make it feel typed, not edited.*

> Hermes is a local first academic triage engine and opportunity radar for one very distractible student (me :)). There is no chat box, no prompt. It starts with FastAPI, polls Google Classroom, Gmail, and opportunity feeds, throws out the noise, extracts real deadlines, checks the web when an opportunity date is missing, and writes everything to SQLite. The dashboard reads that state.

*Why it works: The technical explanation is one long comma-linked chain. The self-aware aside lands before the specs. It does not sound like a product page.*
